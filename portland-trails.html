Mapping Trail Proximity in Portland

Measuring how much of Portland’s Tri-Metro region lies within half a mile of existing trails, and communicating the results through a map, summary statistics, and charts.

The question

How much land in Portland’s Tri-Metro region lies near existing trails, and how does that proximity vary among Clackamas, Multnomah, and Washington counties?

I developed this project for a GEOG 455 infographic assignment, combining spatial analysis with a layout designed to make the main findings easy to compare.

Approach

  1. Selected existing trails from Oregon Metro’s Regional Land Information System (RLIS) trail data.
  2. Created a half-mile buffer around the selected trails and combined overlapping areas to avoid double-counting.
  3. Measured the buffered area within the Tri-Metro study boundary and summarized it by county.
  4. Compared each county’s contribution to the total trail-adjacent area with the percentage of its own study-area land that falls near trails.

What the analysis shows

Approximately 201,176 acres, or 67.3% of the study region, lie within half a mile of existing trails. Multnomah accounts for the largest share of that trail-adjacent area.

County comparisons within the Tri-Metro study boundary
County Share of total trail-adjacent area County study area near trails
Clackamas 21.9% 56.9%
Multnomah 50.2% 71.5%
Washington 27.9% 69.9%

These comparisons answer different questions. A county can contribute a large share of the region’s buffered land without having the highest percentage of its own land near trails. The infographic uses separate charts to distinguish those measures.

Proximity and access

The half-mile buffer measures straight-line proximity to trail features. It does not measure walking routes, trailhead locations, public entry points, or barriers such as rivers and highways. The percentages describe land area within the study boundary, rather than the percentage of residents who can access a trail.

Communicating the results

I paired the regional map with three prominent summary statistics and two county-level charts. A consistent purple accent connects the trail theme across the layout, while terrain, water, and vegetation provide geographic context.

The pie chart shows how the total trail-adjacent area is distributed among counties. The bar chart compares the percentage of each county’s study-area land near trails.

Data and course context

Created for GEOG 455 at UNC–Chapel Hill. Data sources include Oregon Metro RLIS trails and county boundaries, together with GEOG 455 course files. This is a student coursework project.
